The $1.7 Trillion Question

The Trilemma Factor

Global trade is structurally gridlocked. The financier, vendor, and buyer each have conflicting core fears that create a systemic trust gap.

The Financier

Has Capital, No Visibility

Wants to finance trade corridors to earn interest, but completely lacks on-ground performance oversight.

✕ Won't lend without performance proof

The Vendor

Has Product, No Funding

Needs upfront capital to raw materials and start manufacturing, but lacks bankable balance sheet collateral.

✕ Can't access capital without collateral

The Buyer

Has Money, No Guarantee

Ready to purchase, but holds zero legal or physical guarantees that the custom cargo will actually be delivered.

✕ Won't pay without delivery assurance

The Core Insight

Each party's fear creates the other's problem. The financier won't lend because the vendor can't prove performance. The vendor can't perform because the financier won't lend. The buyer won't pay because neither side can guarantee the outcome.

It's not a money problem — it's a trust infrastructure problem.

The Architecture of Unbreakable Trust: Dual-Collateral Mechanics

By forcing both parties to have skin in the game, Escrow Sonet mathematically eliminates fraud before it can even start. No banks, no borders, no counterparty risk.

STAGE 1: THE BUYER DEPOSIT

Buyer Contract Deposit

The buyer deposits the invoice amount plus separate collateral of 30% into the escrow contract. The invoice amount and collateral are accounted for independently.

STAGE 2: THE SELLER DEPOSIT

Vendor Contract Collateral

The vendor's collateral value is recorded as 30% of the invoice amount. It is kept separate from invoice value; this mock phase does not collect either amount.
